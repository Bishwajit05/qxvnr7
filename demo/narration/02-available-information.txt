The controls specify a decision date and a cutoff in London time. Nine o'clock is a configurable demonstration assumption, rather than a claim that every market participant has the same deadline. The publication timeline lets a reviewer move back to an earlier checkpoint. For each delivery hour, the backend selects the latest forecast published by that checkpoint. An update released later cannot silently improve the earlier decision. For this case, the latest eligible publication is seven thirty, with the previous revision at four thirty. Earlier publications may cover fewer next day hours, and the application reports that difference. Before revealing the outcome, the forecast view answers a simple question: what wind generation could the buyer actually have expected, using the information recorded as published at the time?
