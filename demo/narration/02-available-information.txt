The controls specify a decision date and a cutoff in London time. Nine o'clock is a configurable demonstration assumption, rather than a universal market deadline. For every delivery hour, the backend selects the latest forecast published at or before the selected checkpoint. An update released later cannot enter an earlier decision, even when the source data is freshly retrieved today. The publication timeline lets us inspect earlier eligible revisions. Some early publications cover fewer next day hours, and that difference is reported. Actual generation remains clearly retrospective: it is visible to evaluate this historical decision, not presented as information that the buyer possessed in advance.
