A newer forecast does not automatically improve every error measure. In the preserved example, selected mean absolute error was seven hundred and eighty two point seven five megawatts, compared with seven hundred and thirty six point two nine for the previous revisions. That is six point three percent higher error across the same twenty four hours. Eight hours improved and sixteen worsened. Yet positive overforecast energy was lower: around thirteen thousand six hundred and seventeen megawatt hours, compared with fourteen thousand five hundred and forty eight. These statistics answer different questions. The comparison exposes its common sample size. This retrospective case is evidence to inspect, not a rule that older forecasts should always be preferred.
