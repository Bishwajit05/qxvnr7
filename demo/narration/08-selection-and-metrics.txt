The central rule is that a forecast publication must be at or before the selected decision checkpoint. Selection is performed independently for every target hour, because coverage can differ across publications. The previous forecast is the previous distinct eligible publication for that same target. Missing observations remain null, while a genuine zero remains a valid measurement. Headline accuracy uses every complete selected forecast and actual match. A revision comparison uses only hours where both revisions and the actual are present. This prevents a change in sample coverage from masquerading as an improvement. Mean absolute error describes typical error magnitude. Root mean squared error emphasizes large misses. Bias retains direction. Positive error energy sums only positive hourly errors, multiplied by one hour. Publication time is explicitly an availability proxy; the source cannot prove when a particular recipient received each update.
